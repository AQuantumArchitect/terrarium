# Disaster recovery and mitigation funding: DR-4769, DR-4856 and other recent SoCal declarations

**Built:** Nov 11, 2026, evening (PT). Internal research only. Nobody was contacted and nothing was applied for.
**Scope:** Post-disaster mitigation and recovery money and groups for DR-4769-CA (Feb 2024 storms, declared Apr 13, 2024), DR-4856-CA (Jan 2025 LA fires), and the other recent California declarations that cover Ventura, Santa Barbara or LA counties.
**Builds on, does not repeat:** `grants-and-fiscal-sponsors.md` (Listos, CA Fire Foundation × Edison fall grant, VCCF, SGC CRC, BRIC, Community Partners), `ready-gov-alignment.md`, `partners-municipal.md` and `partners-municipal-2.md` (VC VOAD, Ready VC, CERT, 2-1-1, LA County Hubs of Resilience, USDN hubs). Those entries are only cross-referenced here.
**Rules used:** Amounts and dates come from a saved public source. Anything not confirmed is marked **unverified**. OpenFEMA figures are federal share obligated as of the dataset's `lastRefresh` date, so they will grow.
**Raw data:** `drf-raw/` next to this file holds the OpenFEMA pulls (`ca_decls.json`, `hmgp_sum.json`, `paapp_*.json`, `pafp_*.json`, `pa_agg_socal.json`), the DR-4856 HMGP NOFO text, and the Cal OES HMA presentation text.

## Top recommendations

1. **California Fire Foundation: Wildfire Disaster Relief & Recovery, LA and Ventura counties. It's open now and fits best (5).** It funds nonprofits planning relief projects in LA and Ventura counties. One of its five focus areas is "Recovery Planning, Public Education, and/or Community Outreach Campaigns." Typical awards are $25k–$100k. Applications are reviewed quarterly, and the current window runs Oct 1 – Dec 31 with awards in January. This is a **different grant** from the fall Edison grant already in `grants-and-fiscal-sponsors.md`. Terrarium would apply through a fiscal sponsor once one is signed. Whether a fiscally sponsored project is eligible is **unverified**, so ask cffgrants@cpf.org when the time comes. The best fit is a neighbor-preparedness outreach program in burn-scar or flood-prone neighborhoods.
2. **Community Partners is already the fiscal sponsor of the Eaton Fire Collaborative and its long-term recovery group (EFC-LTRG).** The Collaborative's site says so. That makes it real precedent: Community Partners hosts post-fire, neighbor-led recovery coordination. Two uses: (a) cite it in the Community Partners pre-application (Feb 1, 2027 window, per the earlier file); (b) treat the EFC-LTRG as a sister project. Its Collaboratory and its "Community Networks for Mutual Support" page fit Terrarium's neighbor-network idea closely.
3. **HMGP: Terrarium can't be the subapplicant, but it can be the outreach partner on a city's or county's 5% Initiative project (4).** DR-4856 has **$744.7M** in project funds at the 12-month lock-in. That includes a **$74.5M "5% Initiative" target**, which covers public awareness campaigns, warning systems and hazard mapping with no benefit-cost analysis required. Per OpenFEMA, **$0 was obligated** as of Aug 21, 2026. The DR-4856 NOI window closed May 29, 2025. Cal OES now lists **"HMGP – Rolling Application: Open, Continuously,"** and its 2026 deck says NOIs are accepted on a rolling basis. So a city, county or eligible nonprofit that owns a facility can still file an NOI. It would be considered for "the next available funding opportunity"; which pot that means is **unverified**. Private nonprofits are eligible only if they own or operate an eligible PNP facility (44 CFR 206.221(e) / 206.434), and there's a 25% local match. Terrarium's role would be a subcontracted outreach or education partner named in a jurisdiction's 5% Initiative subapplication, or a participant in a local hazard mitigation plan (LHMP) update.
4. **Hold off on CDBG-DR, SBA and Prepare California.** No CDBG-DR allocation exists for the LA fires: Congress hasn't appropriated one, and LA County is only preparing an Action Plan. SBA deadlines have passed and SBA only lends. Prepare California (JumpStart and Match) was a 2021-22 state budget program that the Cal OES page describes in the past tense. Community Power Resiliency was last funded in FY 2020-21 and only went to governments and special districts. Fit is 0–1 for all of them today.
5. **Long-term recovery groups (LTRGs) are the warm doors, and none need a legal vehicle to join as a participant (4–5).**
   - Eaton Fire Collaborative LTRG
   - Palisades LTRG (Pali LTRG)
   - Malibu Community LTRG
   - Topanga REACH
   - LARCRO (LA Region Community Recovery Organization)
   - ENLA (LA County's VOAD)
   - 211 LA Crisis Care Plan Alliance
   - Ventura County Long Term Disaster Recovery Group (Mountain Fire)
   - VCCF Disaster Resilience Initiative

   All of these publicly invite organizations to register, join or partner. The first ask fits the existing partner maps: offer neighbor-preparedness workshops or the 12-domain checklist as a resource, not the store.
6. **Coordinate before anyone contacts a PA applicant.** Many of these applicants are also prospects for Luke's sister project Civic Papers. Agree on one owner per contact so a city or church doesn't hear from two projects in the same week.

**What HMGP pays for that's close to Terrarium (DR-4856 NOFO):**
- **5% Initiative:** warning systems, "mitigation public awareness campaigns," and hazard identification and mapping (including flood after fire). No benefit-cost analysis; up to 48 months.
- **Projects:**
  - secondary power sources (Cal OES ran a "Backup Power/Generator" subapplication webinar on Aug 20, 2025)
  - wildfire-smoke HVAC and filtration in public buildings
  - retrofitting existing shelters and community centers (new shelter builds aren't eligible)
  - defensible space and home-hardening grant programs
- **Funding priority 6:** "Activities that foster a holistic approach to community resilience – partnering across Federal, State, tribal, local governments, non-profits, and the private sector."
- **Microgrids** aren't named in the NOFO. Treat "secondary power sources" and the "Energy Resilience" line in the Cal OES 2026 deck as the closest fit; microgrid eligibility is **unverified**.
- **Resilience hubs** aren't named either. Retrofitting an existing community center as a hub (power, smoke filtration) fits the listed project types; new construction doesn't.
- **Who can apply:**
  - cities, counties, special districts, state agencies and tribes
  - PNPs "consistent with 44 CFR §206.221 and 206.434," meaning those that own or operate an eligible facility. The county or city where the project sits must have an LHMP.
  - Everyone pays a 25% local match.
- **Technical assistance:** ResilientCA@caloes.ca.gov (for a future partner city, not for us to email now).
- **Rolling NOIs:** Cal OES's Grant Opportunities page lists "HMGP – Rolling Application | Open | Continuously." The CFCC 2026 deck says "Cal OES accepts Notices of Interest (NOIs) on a rolling basis" and expects FMA '27, BRIC '27 and PDM '28 in spring 2027. Sources: https://www.caloes.ca.gov/office-of-the-director/operations/recovery-directorate/hazard-mitigation/hm-grant-opportunities/ ; https://cfcc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/CFCC-2026-Cal-OES-HMA-Presentation-Updated.pdf
- **The money is slow.** The Governor's office (Aug 4, 2026) said California had submitted more than $1.5B in FEMA reimbursement claims for the January 2025 fires, and FEMA had obligated about $37M (about 2.5%). That matches the $0 HMGP obligation above. Expect years before any 5% Initiative subaward reaches a partner. https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/08/04/governor-newsom-invites-president-trump-to-deliver-overdue-wildfire-recovery-funding-during-los-angeles-visit/
