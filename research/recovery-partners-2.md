# Recovery partners, round 2: recovery groups, VOADs, resilience centers, hazard mitigation plans

**Built:** Nov 11, 2026, evening (PT). This is research only. Nobody was contacted, nothing was applied for, and no form was submitted.
**Scope:** Six questions that follow up on `disaster-recovery-funding.md`, `grants-and-fiscal-sponsors.md`, `partners-municipal*.md` and `ready-gov-alignment.md`. Anything those files already cover is cross-referenced, not repeated.
**Rules used:**
- Every date and amount comes from a page or dataset read tonight.
- Anything not confirmed is marked **unverified**.
- Federal data is called **OpenFEMA**. The "OpenFEMA call list" is a 150-row list of small FEMA Public Assistance (PA) applicants pulled from OpenFEMA on Nov 11, 2026. Its 34 California rows are all DR-4856 (January 2025 LA fires) applicants in LA County.

**Fit scale:** 5 = act on it now or next; 1 = file for later.

## Top recommendations

1. **The draft of Ventura County's 2027 Multi-Jurisdictional Hazard Mitigation Plan (MJHMP) opens for public comment on Nov 18, 2026, for three weeks. Fit 5, and it's the most time-sensitive item.**
   - The same evening, Nov 18 from 6:30 to 8:00 pm, there's a combined public workshop and steering committee meeting, in person in Camarillo and online.
   - Final pre-submission review is Dec 16, 2026, and plan adoption is targeted for March 2027.
   - All 10 Ventura County cities plus the unincorporated county are planning partners. So this **one document is also the city plan for every Ventura County city**; there are no separate city plans to chase.
   - The county page explicitly invites "residents, businesses, and community organizations."
   - **Plug-in:** a short public comment asking that the mitigation strategy name neighbor-level preparedness networks and resilience hubs as actions. HMGP project and 5% Initiative eligibility leans on what the plan names, so this plants a hook for later hub and outreach money.
   - Luke would have to submit it himself. A draft can be prepared for his review.
   - https://ready.venturacounty.gov/county-plans/hmp-update-2026/
2. **Join VC-VOAD as a "Community Ally". It's open to any group, no 501(c)(3) needed. Fit 5.**
   - VC-VOAD has three tiers:
     - Partner: nonprofits and 501(c)(3)s
     - Associate: government, private sector, CERT
     - Community Ally: "any interested organization, non-profit, agency, group, public agency, or business operating within Ventura County," for discussion, training and information
   - General membership meetings are quarterly and open to the community. The 2026 dates were Mar 26, May 28 and Aug 27, with the fourth TBD.
   - The VOAD is also the doorway to the Ventura County Long Term Disaster Recovery Group (VC LTDRG). Organizations register through a short form on the VC-VOAD Mountain Fire page.
   - https://www.vcvoad.org/join-vc-voad
3. **California Fire Foundation (CFF) accepts fiscally sponsored applicants, with the sponsor as lead. The LA/Ventura page itself is silent on this. Fit 4, rising to 5 once a sponsor is signed.**
   - CFF's General Grant FAQ lists "Nonprofit Organizations (501(c)(3) or via fiscal sponsor)." It says the fiscal sponsor must be the lead applicant, documents must be in the sponsor's legal name, sponsorship must last the whole grant, and sponsor fees must be itemized in the budget.
   - CFF's open Prop 4 page says the same: "if your project is fiscally sponsored, the application must be submitted through the fiscal sponsor's grant portal."
   - The LA/Ventura grant's own page doesn't mention fiscal sponsors, so confirmation for **that specific grant** is still **unverified**. It's very likely, given CFF's stated policy.
   - Details on the LA/Ventura page:
     - Typical awards are $25k–$100k.
     - The windows listed are Jan 1–Mar 31, Jul 1–Sep 30 and Oct 1–Dec 31. There's no Apr–Jun window on this page.
   - Realistic path: apply through Community Partners in a 2027 window once the sponsorship agreement is in place.
4. **SGC Community Resilience Centers (CRC): a fiscally sponsored project has already won as Lead Applicant. Round 2 is 17× oversubscribed, and no Round 3 money exists yet. Fit 3.**
   - Round 1 precedent: "Santa Ana Building Healthy Communities, **a fiscally sponsored project of Charitable Ventures**" won a $500k Planning Grant.
   - Round 2 drew $935M in requests for $55M of funding.
   - The Round 2 guidelines' FAQ says "Funding is currently only available for Round 2."
   - Best near-term role: a programming partner to past awardees.
     - **Ojai Valley Fire Safe Council** (Ventura; Round 1 Planning Grant). Its planned hubs include islanded microgrids, mass care and year-round programs.
     - **St. John's Community Health** (LA; Round 1 $10M Implementation Grant; also an ENLA member).
5. **Eaton Fire Collaborative (EFC) member meetings are on the second Tuesday of every month, and membership is by an online form. Fit 4.**
   - EFC is fiscally sponsored by Community Partners.
   - Its Collaboratory co-locates more than 200 partner organizations.
   - Its Unmet Needs process went live in October 2026.
   - **Plug-in:** offer the neighbor-network and preparedness checklist as a resource at a member meeting. It also serves as Community Partners precedent (already noted in the earlier file).
6. **LA County: ENLA is the seat at the hazard-mitigation table. Fit 3, long-horizon.**
   - ENLA sat on the external stakeholder group of LA County's 2025 All-Hazards Mitigation Plan advisory committee.
   - The plan commits to making that committee **standing** and made up of "representatives from diverse community groups."
   - The next update kicks off around **November 2028**.
   - ENLA voting membership needs a 501(c)(3), so this waits for the sponsor.
7. **Cross-check result: hardly any VOAD or recovery-group member organizations show up as FEMA PA private nonprofits.** Only **AltaMed** (an EFC Collaboratory on-site partner; DR-4856, $10,000, Category B) is a same-entity match. Several Altadena and Palisades congregations on the OpenFEMA call list are **denominational kin** of VOAD members (United Methodist, Presbyterian, Episcopal), not the same legal entities. Table in §2.
8. **Hub-type hardening is happening, but mostly through HMGP (Section 404) generators, not Section 406.**
   - OpenFEMA records 406 mitigation dollars on seven California call-list entities, but it doesn't say what the measures are. So "backup power through 406" is **unverified** for all of them.
   - Pending HMGP generator subapplications exist for:
     - Malibu and Crescenta Valley Water District (both on the call list)
     - Simi Valley (Ventura; its OES is a VC-VOAD associate)
     - Downey (a recent ENLA member)
     - LA County (two records)
   - All are **Pending with $0 obligated**.
