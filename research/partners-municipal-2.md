# Municipal and civic partners for Terrarium, pass two

**Research date:** Wednesday, November 11, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)
**Builds on:** `partners-municipal.md` (pass one). Pass one covered about 40 programs, mostly in Ventura County, plus Santa Barbara County OEM, LA County OEM, RYLAN, EmpowerLA/NCEPA, Listos California, the Strategic Growth Council, USDN Resilience Hubs, Washington Map Your Neighborhood, Ready New York and Transition US. **None of those are repeated here.** Where a program here relies on one of them, the row says so.
**Scope of this pass:** (a) the rest of Southern and Central California and some Bay Area standouts; (b) city and county neighborhood-preparedness programs across the country; (c) national bodies and networks; (d) libraries and schools acting as resilience hubs; (e) faith-based disaster networks.
**Method:** I used web search plus direct fetches of each official page. Every fact below comes from the cited page or from an official document linked from it. A few city sites (Torrance, Laguna Beach, Oakland, Palo Alto, San José, Riverside, FEMA) block automated fetches. For those, facts come from the search-indexed text of the same official page, and the URL is still the official one. Nothing was invented. **No one was contacted.** Luke sends all outreach himself.
**Contact rule:** Only websites, contact forms and program or organization emails published on official pages are listed. Personal phone numbers and personal email addresses are left out on purpose, even where a page shows them.
**Location rule:** This memo does not name or imply Luke's exact location.

## Read this first: what changed since pass one

- **FEMA's Individual and Community Preparedness Division (ICPD) was shut down in September 2025.** Bloomberg and the Boston Globe reported this in Feb and Mar 2026. CERT is still a FEMA program, now run from a different part of the agency, but the national "Citizen Corps" layer is thin. That makes **local CERT programs, the National CERT Association and state programs (California Volunteers, Listos)** the working doors, not FEMA headquarters. A neighbor-level framework like Terrarium's fills part of the gap ICPD left.
- **Luke's Ready.gov point applies here too.** Ready.gov content is public domain, so Terrarium can reuse it freely. That is not true of everything below. **Map Your Neighborhood is copyrighted by LuAn K. Johnson, PhD** and used by Washington EMD with her permission. Many city workbooks carry their own terms. Rule: reuse Ready.gov freely; **link to** local programs' materials, or ask their permission before adapting them.
- **Washington's Map Your Neighborhood workbooks were discontinued in 2021** (Thurston County page). The videos and online templates remain, and cities like Manhattan Beach still teach MYN.
- **Be Ready Utah already organizes household preparedness into "12 Areas of Preparedness"** (plus "4 Tenets"). That is a public-sector precedent for Terrarium's 12-domain frame and is worth a side-by-side comparison before any outreach.

## Top 20 first conversations

Order reflects how warm the door is, how well it fits the Suburban Gap thesis (households are mostly ready; neighbors aren't coordinated), and whether there's an ask that doesn't need a 501(c)(3). Asks are link exchanges, permission to adapt materials, co-hosted workshops, or Terrarium writing a case study. None of them are store pitches.

1. **Santa Barbara Bucket Brigade.** A volunteer disaster-response group formed after the Jan 9, 2018 Montecito debris flow. It clears mud and debris and runs year-round training and restoration. It's the clearest regional story of neighbors acting as first responders. **Ask:** a case study for Terrarium's story page and a link exchange; later, a co-hosted "what we learned" workshop. **Contact:** [contact page](https://sbbucketbrigade.org/contact-us/), `contactus@sbbucketbrigade.org`. Fit 5.
2. **Topanga Coalition for Emergency Preparedness (TCEP).** An all-volunteer 501(c)(3) that has served Topanga and Malibu for close to 30 years. Its **Neighborhood Network** links groups of about 10–50 households to share contacts, skills, equipment and notifications. It's the closest existing match to Terrarium's coordination layer. **Ask:** an interview and case study; permission to reference their Neighborhood Network setup; a link exchange. **Contact:** [tcep.org](https://tcep.org/), `nn@tcep.org`. Fit 5.
3. **Altagether (Altadena).** Started after the Eaton fire. Volunteer Neighborhood Captains organize block-level zones; search-indexed pages report 150+ captains covering more than 65% of Altadena. Its site warns against using its map to solicit captains or promote services. **Ask:** one respectful email to the organization (not to captains) offering the free 12-domain checklist for their recovery-to-readiness phase. **Contact:** `info@altagether.org` via [altagether.org](https://altagether.org/). Fit 5.
4. **San Francisco Neighborhood Empowerment Network (NEN), Empowered Communities Program.** Helps SF neighborhoods write culturally competent Resilience Action Plans and runs a HUB program. **Ask:** compare their Resilience Action Plan template with the 12-domain checklist; propose a co-authored "plan template for suburbs" piece. **Contact:** [empowersf.org/programs](https://www.empowersf.org/programs/). Fit 5.
5. **SF Fire NERT (Neighborhood Emergency Response Team).** Free neighbor-helping-neighbor training for anyone who lives or works in SF, with neighborhood teams and an advisory board. **Ask:** link exchange; a guest piece on NERT's neighborhood team structure as a model. **Contact:** [sf-fire.org/nert](https://sf-fire.org/nert). Fit 4.
6. **Seattle OEM, READY Together (formerly SNAP) and Community Emergency Hubs.** A three-phase neighborhood organizing kit with a Neighborhood Planning Form, a Household Information Form and a Skills and Equipment Form. The city lists 135 designated Hubs, and the volunteer Seattle Emergency Hubs site maps them. **Ask:** permission to adapt the forms, or link to them as the model for Terrarium's block tools. **Contact:** [Prepare Your Neighborhood](https://www.seattle.gov/emergency-management/prepare/prepare-your-neighborhood); [seattleemergencyhubs.org](https://seattleemergencyhubs.org/). Fit 5.
7. **Oregon OEM "Be 2 Weeks Ready" and Neighbors Ready! (Beaverton).** A state toolkit (eight units) for neighborhood groups. Neighbors Ready! trains volunteer Community Hosts to run the meetings. **Ask:** adopt the Community Host model for Terrarium hosts and link both; a case study. **Contact:** `community.preparedness@oem.oregon.gov` via the [OEM page](https://www.oregon.gov/oem/hazardsprep/pages/2-weeks-ready.aspx); [neighborsready.org](https://www.neighborsready.org/). Fit 5.
8. **Be Ready Utah.** The state program uses "4 Tenets and 12 Areas of Preparedness" and has family, business, school and community tracks plus a public library of handouts. **Ask:** a crosswalk between their 12 Areas and Terrarium's 12 domains, offered to them as a free resource; a link exchange. **Contact:** [beready.utah.gov](https://beready.utah.gov/). Fit 5.
9. **Hawai'i HHARP (Hawai'i Hazards Awareness and Resilience Program).** A six-module community program run by HI-EMA that formally recognizes "Disaster Resilient Communities" (Waimanalo, Hanapēpē-'Ele'ele). **Ask:** use their recognition model as the template for a Terrarium "ready block" milestone; a case study. **Contact:** [dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/hharp](https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/hharp/). Fit 5.
10. **Berkeley Ready and the Berkeley Disaster Preparedness Neighborhood Network (BDPNN).** City classes teach six steps for organizing a neighborhood. Mini-grants let new groups start an emergency-supplies cache and existing groups expand one. **Ask:** case study; cite the cache mini-grant as a model for any future Terrarium micro-grant. **Contact:** [berkeleyca.gov neighborhood page](https://berkeleyca.gov/safety-health/disaster-preparedness/neighborhood); [bdpnn.org](https://www.bdpnn.org/resources/groups/neighborhood-groups). Fit 5.
11. **Palo Alto Neighborhood and Block Preparedness Coordinator (NPC/BPC) program.** Part of the city's Emergency Services Volunteers. Block coordinators build connections and report conditions during emergencies. Training is free, quarterly, and also offered as an online module. **Ask:** adapt the BPC role description for Terrarium's "block steward"; link exchange. **Contact:** `esv@paloalto.gov`; [program page](https://www.paloalto.gov/Departments/Emergency-Services/Emergency-Volunteers/NPCBPC). Fit 5.
12. **Sonoma County COPE (Communities Organized to Prepare for Emergencies).** Neighborhood leaders organize roughly 10–20 homes around shared plans, supplies and communication. The county lists COPE among its neighborhood programs, with active groups in Santa Rosa, Windsor and North County. **Ask:** case study (wildfire-tested suburbs); link exchange. **Contact:** [socoemergency.org neighborhood programs](https://socoemergency.org/get-ready/neighborhood-programs/); [COPE North Sonoma County](https://copenorthernsonomacounty.org/about-us/). Fit 5.
13. **Ready Your Long Beach Neighborhood (RYLBN).** A city program, relaunched during a past National Preparedness Month. Neighborhoods form a group, write a Neighborhood Response Plan, inventory skills and equipment, build a confidential contact list and drill. It mirrors LA's RYLAN in a mid-size suburb-like city. **Ask:** link exchange; offer the 12-domain checklist as a companion. **Contact:** `LongBeach-EOC@longbeach.gov`; [RYLBN page](https://longbeach.gov/disasterpreparedness/disaster-preparedness/ready-your-long-beach-neighborhood/). Fit 4.
14. **Just In Case BH (Beverly Hills).** A city program that organizes residents into zones with Neighborhood Coordinators and Block Captains, plus a published disaster manual. **Ask:** compare the Block Captain role with Terrarium's; link exchange. **Contact:** [Program Roles & Positions](https://www.beverlyhills.org/473/Program-Roles-Positions). Fit 4.
15. **Manhattan Beach CERT Association: Map Your Neighborhood.** MYN has been taught in the city since 2008 under the Fire Department. Blocks report status to CERT reporting locations even when 911 is down. **Ask:** a case study on joining MYN and CERT reporting; link exchange. **Contact:** `info@mbcerta.org`; [mbcerta.org/map-your-neighborhood](https://www.mbcerta.org/map-your-neighborhood). Fit 4.
16. **City of Santa Barbara Fire Department, Office of Emergency Services.** Runs CERT and public outreach (family plans, neighborhood skills). The city-level partner pass one didn't cover (pass one had the county). **Ask:** listing as a neighbor-coordination resource; a 12-domain talk for CERT graduates. **Contact:** [Prepare page](https://santabarbaraca.gov/government/departments/fire-department/office-emergency-services/prepare); [Public Outreach](https://santabarbaraca.gov/government/departments/fire-department/office-emergency-services/public-outreach). Fit 4.
17. **ReadySLO and SLO County CERT (including Teen CERT).** The county preparedness hub plus a 24-hour CERT basic course ($40, ages 15+) and a Teen CERT program. **Ask:** link exchange; a workshop offer for CERT graduates. **Contact:** [readyslo.org Get Involved](https://www.readyslo.org/prepare/get-involved/); [slocountycert.org](https://slocountycert.org/training). Fit 4.
18. **Direct Relief (Santa Barbara).** A California-based medical relief organization. It's a long-time partner of Cal OES (including as a Business Operations Center partner) and of Santa Barbara County's preparedness work; county fire chiefs honored it in Feb 2026. **Ask:** realistic only as a later credibility or referral partner (fit is lower until there's a legal vehicle); for now, cite and link their wildfire guidance. **Contact:** [directrelief.org California fires](https://www.directrelief.org/emergency/california-wildfires/). Fit 3.
19. **UMCOR Connecting Neighbors (United Methodist).** A free congregational training in three modules (Ready Congregants, Ready Churches, Ready Response), run through regional conferences. It's the faith-world twin of Terrarium's household-to-neighborhood ladder. **Ask:** offer the 12-domain checklist as a companion handout; co-host a session through a conference disaster ministry. **Contact:** [Oregon-Idaho Connecting Neighbors](https://www.umoi.org/connectingneighbors) (example conference page). Fit 5.
20. **National CERT Association (NCA).** The national association of CERT volunteers, with a national conference, CERT Week and a resource library; its 2026 award announcement was datelined Irvine, CA. With FEMA's ICPD gone, this is the best national CERT door. **Ask:** submit the 12-domain checklist to the resource library; pitch a conference session on "after CERT class: organizing your block." **Contact:** [nationalcert.org](https://www.nationalcert.org/) (Contact Us page). Fit 4.

## Best programs to copy

These are the strongest blueprints for Terrarium's neighbor-coordination tools. For each, the "copy" column says what to borrow and whether to link, adapt with permission, or reuse freely.

| Program | What to borrow | Reuse status |
|---|---|---|
| **Seattle READY Together / SNAP** | Three phases: (1) meet and discuss; (2) organize (coordinator, meeting place, roles, map signup); (3) drills and exercises. Three forms: Neighborhood Planning, Household Information, Skills and Equipment. Tip sheets on utility control, search and rescue, and damage assessment. | City material; link it and ask before adapting. |
| **Map Your Neighborhood** (WA EMD; taught in Manhattan Beach) | The 9 steps after a disaster; a 15–20 home unit you can canvass in an hour; a neighborhood map of gas and propane; a contact list that flags elderly neighbors, neighbors with disabilities, and kids home alone. | **Copyrighted by LuAn K. Johnson, PhD.** Link to it, or get permission. Workbooks discontinued in 2021. |
| **Palo Alto NPC/BPC** | A two-tier role ladder (neighborhood coordinator over block coordinators) inside a city volunteer corps; quarterly free training plus an online module. | Copy the role design (an idea, not text). |
| **Oregon Be 2 Weeks Ready + Neighbors Ready! Community Hosts** | An eight-unit group toolkit; trained volunteer hosts facilitate. Fits a "Terrarium host" program. | State material; link it and ask. |
| **Be Ready Utah "4 Tenets / 12 Areas"** | A precedent for a 12-part household frame from a state agency. Build a crosswalk table. | Public agency; cite and link. |
| **HHARP (Hawai'i)** | Six modules ending in formal community recognition ("Disaster Resilient Community"). The model for a "ready block" milestone. | Cite it and copy the structure. |
| **Firewise USA (NFPA)** | A recognition recipe: resident committee, a site of 8–2,500 homes, a risk assessment, a three-year action plan, one volunteer hour per home per year, annual renewal. About 3,600 sites in 36 states (2026). | Copy the structure, not the brand. |
| **Berkeley Ready + BDPNN** | Six organizing steps; neighborhood supply caches funded by mini-grants. | Cite it and copy the idea. |
| **TCEP Neighborhood Network** | Groups of 10–50 households with a mass-notification setup TCEP helps configure. | Ask for an interview. |
| **Portland BEECN** | 50 pre-designated radio nodes (mostly parks). After a qualifying quake, two trained volunteers self-deploy to each. Training is 90 minutes. A low-tech "where to report" layer for neighborhoods. | Cite; adapt the concept for GMRS and ham radio domains. |
| **Baltimore Community Resiliency Hubs** | The city partners with 17 trusted, service-based nonprofits (including faith-based ones) in under-resourced neighborhoods. Shows a city resourcing existing groups rather than building new ones. | Case study. |
| **NYC Strengthening Communities** | City grants to networks of 3+ community or faith organizations that write their own emergency plans ($40,000 per network in year one; up to eight networks in the 2026 cohort). Plus NYC's Community Emergency Planning Toolkit. | A model for future grant language. |
| **LDS Stake and Ward Emergency Planning Guide** | Worksheets for disaster review, disruptions, special needs, equipment and skills, services, and assignments. A congregation-scale version of the block inventory. | Church material; link it. |
| **Lutheran Disaster Response guidebook / NDIN "Be a Ready Congregation" tip sheets** | Step-by-step congregational planning; free tip sheets on sheltering, continuity and spiritual care. | Link them; hand them to faith partners. |
| **USDN Resilience Hub principle** (covered in pass one) | Hubs should be led by residents and community or faith groups, with government in support. | Already covered in pass one. |

## Notes for Luke

- **No legal vehicle is needed for the top 20 asks.** All of them are link exchanges, permission requests, case studies or workshop offers. VOAD and COAD membership, NYC-style grants and any formal MOU wait for the fiscal sponsor.
- **Messaging that lands with these partners:** "neighbor coordination after CERT class," "the block between the household kit and the city EOC," and "access and functional needs" (SD County NET, MYN contact lists). Avoid "prepper" framing. Faith partners respond to the widows-and-orphans origin and to caring for elderly and disabled neighbors.
- **Ready.gov as backbone:** most of these programs already cite Ready.gov or FEMA material. Building Terrarium's pages on Ready.gov topics, then linking to these local programs, puts Terrarium inside the same conversation.
- **Stale-date warning:** class dates, cohort sizes and grant amounts above are as published on Nov 11, 2026. Recheck them before quoting any in outreach.
- **Not verified this pass:** a standalone MERRAG website; Church World Service's "Community Arise" (search results pointed to PC(USA) pages, so CWS was left out); and current Neighborhoods USA contacts beyond the site's contact form.
