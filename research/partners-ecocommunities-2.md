# Eco-communities and eco-projects, pass two: beyond California

**Pass:** the rest of the US by region, Canada and international. It follows pass one (`partners-ecocommunities.md`, 87 entries, California-heavy) and does not repeat any of its entries.
**Date of this sweep:** 11 November 2026, America/Los_Angeles.

## How to read this file

Terrarium retrofits existing suburbia into stations of 50–150 people that split when full. It is not bunker isolation and not a hippie commune. It spans 12 capability domains under Father Tree (building) and Mother Mycelium (gifting and coordination). This pass favors suburban retrofit, cohousing inside existing neighborhoods, block-level sharing, splitting and replication, structured mutual aid, and care for elders, disabled adults, foster kids and refugees.

**Fit (1–5)** measures relevance to Terrarium, not quality:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Close match to a Terrarium pattern (retrofit, block-level sharing, split/replication, or a neighborhood care model), with a public method or door. |
| 4 | Strong case study or method source; a step removed (new build, single site, or practitioner org). |
| 3 | Established precedent at station scale (typical cohousing, classic ecovillage, care community), useful but ordinary. |
| 2 | Farther away: rural, income-sharing, spiritual, or a thin public interface. |
| 1 | Watch only: thin web presence or weak fit. |

Every size figure comes from the source cited in that row. "Not stated" means the fetched official page gave no headcount, and none was guessed. Each "Public contact" is a website or contact page only, with no personal emails and no residents named. ## Top 20

Ranked by how close each is to Terrarium (retrofit inside existing neighborhoods, block-level sharing, splitting or replication, structured mutual aid, care for elders, disabled adults, foster kids, refugees), not by fame.

1. **City Repair Project, Portland, OR (fit 5).** Neighbors turn their own intersection into a shared plaza. Share-It Square was the first "intersection repair," and City Repair has 30 years of this work in Portland. It's the closest existing method for a station's Street zone. Angle: adopt the method; content collab. https://cityrepair.org/
2. **Buy Nothing Project, Bainbridge Island, WA (fit 5).** 14M+ members in 50+ countries. Groups "sprout" into smaller local groups at roughly 800–1,000 members. It is Mother Mycelium gifting and "split when full" already working at scale. Angle: link exchange; content on gifting as preparedness. https://buynothingproject.org/
3. **Abundant Community Edmonton, AB (fit 5).** A Block Connector visits about 20 households on their own block, and Neighbourhood Connectors support them, all run through community leagues with city backing. It's a ready-made template for a station's first step. Angle: adopt the method. https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/documents/PDF/ACE-BlockConnectorsResourceGuide.pdf
4. **Munksøgård, Roskilde, Denmark (fit 5).** 100 homes in five groups of 20, each with its own common house, with mixed tenure (owners, co-op shares, and rental groups for youth, families and seniors). It's the clearest built picture of several stations side by side. Angle: case study; adopt the structure. https://www.munksoegaard.dk/
5. **Kailash Ecovillage, Portland, OR (fit 5).** About 50 people in a retrofitted 1959 apartment complex with a food forest, 68 kW of solar and permitted greywater. It shows an ordinary existing building converted in place. Angle: case study; visit. https://www.kailashecovillage.org/
6. **Ross Chapin Architects / pocket neighborhoods, Langley, WA (fit 5).** The originator of 8–12 homes around a shared commons, built under cottage-housing codes inside single-family zoning. It's the physical grammar for Hearth and Street zones. Angle: adopt the design; link to the public hub. https://rosschapin.com/
7. **Transition Town Totnes, UK (fit 5).** The origin of Transition. Its Transition Streets project reached about 500 households through neighbor groups on the same street. Angle: adopt Transition Streets; content collab. https://www.transitiontowntotnes.org/
8. **Bridge Meadows, Oregon (fit 5).** Foster and kinship families and elders 55+ live in the same community: 3 communities, about 211–225 residents, with Tacoma opening in 2027. It speaks directly to the orphans and elderly in Terrarium's story. Angle: adopt the care model; case study. https://bridgemeadows.org/
9. **Treehouse Easthampton, MA (fit 5).** 60 affordable homes on a cul-de-sac where older adults act as "honorary grandparents" to children leaving foster care. The Foundation promotes the model for replication. Angle: same as Bridge Meadows. https://www.treehousefoundation.org/
10. **Village to Village Network, US (fit 5).** 245 aging-in-place villages in 37 states plus DC. Neighbors organize elder support where they already live, and Beacon Hill Village (Boston, 2002) started it. It's the model for how one Terrarium method can spread to hundreds of places. Angle: adopt the method. https://www.vtvnetwork.org/
11. **Dunbar/Spring Neighborhood Foresters, Tucson, AZ (fit 5).** An existing neighborhood retrofitted block by block: 1,700+ native trees and rain gardens harvesting 1M+ gallons a year. Pair it with Brad Lancaster's tours and Watershed Management Group. Angle: case study for the water domain; content collab. https://dunbarspringneighborhoodforesters.org/
12. **Repair Café International (fit 5).** 3,826 Repair Cafés in 50+ countries at the end of 2025, all running from a free starter kit. It's the best proof that a free method replicates. Angle: adopt the method; link exchange. https://www.repaircafe.org/en/
13. **Andelssamfundet i Hjortshøj, Aarhus, Denmark (fit 5).** It has grown to 8 housing groups since 1986 by adding a group at a time. It shows replication within one place. Angle: case study. https://www.andelssamfundet.dk/
14. **Cleveland EcoVillage, OH (fit 4).** An ecovillage made from an existing city neighborhood around a rail station, now carried by Northwest Neighborhoods CDC. Angle: case study. https://www.nwneighborhoods.org/ecovillage
15. **Earthsong Eco-Neighbourhood, Auckland, NZ (fit 4).** 32 homes and about 68 residents inside a suburb, not out of town. Angle: case study. https://www.earthsong.org.nz/
16. **Community First! Village, Austin, TX (fit 4).** 522 residents, 440 of them formerly homeless. Homes are grouped in pockets of 40–50 around shared spaces, and the site is expanding from 51 to 178 acres. Angle: case study; public tours. https://mlf.org/community-first/
17. **EVA-Lanxmeer, Culemborg, Netherlands (fit 4).** A district of about 350 homes steered by its residents' association. Angle: case study. https://lanxmeer.nl/
18. **Hope Meadows, Rantoul, IL (fit 4).** The 1994 original of the foster-kids-plus-elders neighborhood. A frank FIC interview covers its funding collapse and pivot, which is useful for planning. Angle: case study. https://hopemeadows.org/
19. **Strong Towns, US (fit 4).** The mainstream case for incremental, financially resilient towns: 6,500+ members and 290+ local conversations, a ready audience for suburban retrofit. Angle: content collab; link exchange. https://www.strongtowns.org/
20. **Sarvodaya Shramadana, Sri Lanka (fit 4).** Village self-help in 15,000+ villages over 60+ years, using shared-labor work days (shramadana). It's the largest village-to-village replication on record. Angle: adopt methods. https://www.sarvodaya.org/

## Methods worth borrowing

### Replication and splitting
- **Sprouting (Buy Nothing).** Groups split into smaller local groups at roughly 800–1,000 members to keep things personal. This is the closest real-world rule to "stations split when full." Source: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ottawa/ottawa-buy-nothing-groups-1.4522255
- **Sub-communities of 20 (Munksøgård, Hjortshøj).** Several housing groups share a site, each with its own common house. New groups are added instead of growing one group past its limit. Sources: https://www.munksoegaard.dk/bogrupper/ ; https://www.andelssamfundet.dk/hvem-er-vi
- **Pockets of 40–50 (Community First! Village).** Homes are grouped around shared spaces in pockets of 40–50. That's a working size for a Hearth-to-Street cluster. Source: https://mlf.org/community-first/
- **Free starter kits (Repair Café, Transition, Village to Village).** Each spread to hundreds or thousands of places by giving away a startup guide and keeping a light central hub. That matches Terrarium's free frameworks. Sources: https://www.repaircafe.org/en/ ; https://transitionnetwork.org/ ; https://www.vtvnetwork.org/
- **Adjacent sister communities (Sunward and Great Oak in Ann Arbor; Cully Grove and Cully Green in Portland).** A second community forms right beside the first and shares relationships.
- **State-backed replication (Senegal ecovillage program; Sarvodaya).** Existing villages are retrofitted, not replaced.

### Governance and block structure
- **Block Connectors (Abundant Community Edmonton).** One volunteer per block of about 20 households, with a Neighbourhood Connector above. This could be Terrarium's first role.
- **Intersection repair (City Repair).** Neighbors who share a corner design it together, which builds a decision-making group before any housing changes.
- **Transition Streets (Totnes).** Groups of neighboring households on one street work through a shared workbook on energy, water and food.
- **Pocket-neighborhood design (Ross Chapin).** 8–12 homes face a shared commons, with parking at the edge. It needs a cottage-housing code, so a station may need to work with the city.
- **Resident-controlled land (Dudley Street; Troy Gardens/MACLT; La Borda's 75-year municipal lease; LILAC's Mutual Home Ownership Society).** These keep shared land and homes affordable and community-held.
- **Time-bank ledgers (hOurworld; Transition Fujino's Yorozuya passbook).** These track reciprocal help without money. In Yorozuya, balances start at zero and both parties sign each exchange. Either could be Mother Mycelium's bookkeeping.

### Care models (widows, orphans, elders, disabled adults, refugees)
- **Intergenerational foster neighborhoods (Hope Meadows → Bridge Meadows → Treehouse).** Elders 55+ live beside foster and kinship families as mentors and "honorary grandparents," with staff support. Hope Meadows' funding history is the cautionary tale: it relied on state foster-care grants and had to change once those shrank. Source: https://www.ic.org/communiversity-library/article/living-together-an-interview-about-reimagining-community/
- **Aging-in-place Villages (Beacon Hill Village, Village to Village Network).** Elders stay in their own houses with help from volunteers and members. It fits suburban retrofit because it needs no new building.
- **Neighborhood nursing (Buurtzorg).** Self-managing teams of up to 12 nurses build networks of family and neighbors around each client.
- **Shared-life disability care (L'Arche, Camphill, Innisfree, First Place Phoenix).** These range from shared homes to farm villages to supported apartments in a city.
- **Refugee land access (Global Gardens, Boise).** It gives refugee families land, training and market access. Note that the US private refugee-sponsorship program (Welcome Corps) was terminated in February 2025, so refugee care currently runs through resettlement agencies and programs like this one. Source: https://welcome.us/explainers/latest-changes-to-refugee-admissions-and-the-welcome-corps
- **Elder day care inside a community (Kommune Niederkaufungen's Tagespflege Lossetal).** It serves up to 15 people with dementia.
- **Women-led ecovillage for displaced people (Nashira, Colombia).**

## Stale or flagged (not counted above)

- **Welcome Corps (US private refugee sponsorship).** Terminated 26 Feb 2025. It takes no new applications. Source: https://welcome.us/explainers/latest-changes-to-refugee-admissions-and-the-welcome-corps
- **Seoul Sharing City.** The city program was largely scaled back after 2022 and its funding withdrawn. The Share Hub info platform remains. Treat it as a historical method only. Source: https://www.iiiee.lu.se/sites/iiiee.lu.se/files/2024-05/Urban%20Sharing%20in%20Seoul%20-%20City%20report%20no%205.pdf
- **Senegal ANEV.** The agency was merged in 2019 into ASERGMV, which still runs an ecovillage directorate. The 14,000-village target dates from 2009–2015 and its progress was not verified. It is listed in the table under ASERGMV.
- **generationsofhope.org.** The domain now serves gambling spam. Hope Meadows is live at https://hopemeadows.org/ instead. Do not link the old domain.
- **huehuecoyotl.net.** The domain is hijacked (it serves a sports-streaming site). Use the GEN listing instead.
- **avalonvillage.org.** It lapsed (Wix error). Avalon Village is live at https://www.theavalonvillage.org/.
- **Idaho.** Little turned up beyond Boise refugee farming. There's Gold Light Ranch, Sandpoint (an FIC listing of 11 people; no official site verified), Dream River Ranch, Oasis (only a third-party listing), Project Farmland, Meridian (a nonprofit pilot, not yet a community: https://projectfarmland.org/), and several forming-only groups (Sovereign Way, Village: Caritatis). None is counted.
- **Pioneer Valley Cohousing, Amherst, MA; Northeast Seattle Tool Library.** Their known URLs failed (500 error or redirect) on 11 Nov 2026. They were not verified and are not counted.
- **Acorn Community.** Its own domain didn't respond. It's listed through the Federation of Egalitarian Communities page and Southern Exposure's page.
- **Ecoaldea Atlántida, Otamatea, Piracanga, Damanhur.** These are live but thin or a weak fit, so they're kept at fit 1–2 as watch items.
